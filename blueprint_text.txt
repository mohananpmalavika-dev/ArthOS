CHAPTER 06
Why Existing Finance Products Fail
A structural critique of the current financial product landscape.
The industry assumes that access to financial products solves financial problems. It does not. Access to
products without the cognition to use them well produces worse outcomes, not better ones.
Five Structural Failures
F1 · Products solve for transactions, not humans.
Every major financial product is built around a transaction type. The human making those transactions is an
afterthought. No product has a model of the human at its core.
F2 · Information is mistaken for intelligence.
Dashboards produce enormous financial information. But information without interpretation is noise. People need
clarity about what it means, not more data.
F3 · Behaviour is tracked but never improved.
Many apps track spending. Almost none actively work to improve it. Knowing you spent n8,000 on dining is
different from understanding why, and what to do instead.
F4 · Products are designed for the financially literate.
Most products assume a user who already understands finance. This excludes the vast majority of the population
— and almost the entirety of emerging markets.
F5 · No product operates on future-state thinking.
Financial products are backward-looking. They tell you what happened. None tell you what is about to happen if
nothing changes — the only information that truly motivates behavioural change.

CHAPTER 07
Behaviour · Awareness · Stability
The three dimensions of true financial health.
SANKHYA identified three dimensions that together constitute genuine financial health. They are not about
wealth. They are about readiness, resilience, and rationality.
BEHAVIOUR — 40% Weight
Financial behaviour is the aggregate of all financial decisions a person makes, habituated over time. It includes
spending discipline, savings consistency, debt management, and the degree to which short-term impulses override
long-term goals. Behaviour is not determined by income. It is determined by habit, identity, and awareness.
Spending discipline · Savings consistency · Debt management patterns
Impulse vs. planned purchases · Financial habit formation
AWARENESS — 30% Weight
Financial awareness is the accuracy of a person's mental model of their own financial reality. Most people operate
with a significantly distorted picture — overestimating income, underestimating expenses, misunderstanding risk.
Awareness is not intelligence. It is calibration.
Accuracy of self-assessed financial position · Understanding of existing risks
Knowledge of future consequences · Awareness of financial blind spots
STABILITY — 30% Weight
Financial stability is not about having money. It is about being resilient to disruption. Stability is the capacity to
survive uncertainty — financially, emotionally, and practically.
Emergency fund adequacy · Income source diversity · Fixed obligation ratio
Recovery time from financial shock · Insurance adequacy

The Survival Engine
Your first real financial reality check.
"If my income stopped today —
how long could I continue living my current lifestyle?"
Survival Window Calculation
MONTHLY ESSENTIAL EXPENSES
Fixed obligations + Minimum living costs
LIQUID AVAILABLE ASSETS
Savings + Accessible emergency funds
SURVIVAL WINDOW (DAYS)
( Liquid Assets ÷ Monthly Expenses ) × 30
Why the Survival Engine Works
Visceral awareness.
Telling someone their window is 18 days is more motivating
than any graph of monthly spending.
Human-scale thinking.
People do not relate to "net worth". They relate to "how many
days can I survive".
Universal across incomes.
Whether earning n15,000 or n1,50,000/month — the
Survival Window question hits equally hard.
Urgency without alarm.
The Survival Engine does not manipulate. It shows the truth.
The truth itself is motivating.

CHAPTER 14
Financial Cognition Layer
Where ARTH.OS moves from behaviour tracking to mind modelling.
The Financial Cognition Layer is what separates ARTH.OS from every other financial product in existence.
The system stops describing what a person does with money, and starts understanding why.
What the Cognition Layer Models
Money Beliefs
Core beliefs about wealth — is money scarce or abundant? Is it a tool or an identity marker? These beliefs, often
formed in childhood, determine financial behaviour more than any external factor.
Cognitive Bias Profile
ARTH.OS identifies which biases most affect financial decisions — present bias, loss aversion, optimism bias,
sunk-cost fallacy, anchoring. Naming the bias is the first step to neutralising it.
Risk Perception Calibration
Most people's risk perception is systematically distorted. ARTH.OS calibrates a user's risk perception against
objective probability, identifying where they over- or under-estimate.
Financial Emotional Triggers
What events or emotional states drive impulsive financial decisions? What creates financial anxiety? What creates
false confidence? The Cognition Layer maps and tracks these triggers.
"Understanding why someone makes a financial decision is worth
more than knowing what decision they made."

CHAPTER 22
Risk Assessment
Honest assessment of what could go wrong — and how we think about it.
SANKHYA is built on the belief that honest risk assessment is a prerequisite for building durable companies. These
risks are real, material, and require active mitigation.
HIGH Assessment Completion Drop-off
Risk: Users abandon the BAS™ Assessment before completion, preventing score generation.
Mitigation: UX optimisation. Progressive disclosure. Emotional hooks. Salary Roast as completion incentive.
HIGH Data Quality in SMS Parsing
Risk: SMS transaction data is inconsistently formatted across banks, leading to poor signal quality.
Mitigation: Conservative parsing with human-verified training data. Graceful degradation to survey signals.
MEDIUM Low Day-30 Retention
Risk: Users engage once and do not return, limiting behavioural data accumulation.
Mitigation: Weekly score updates. Personalised check-ins. Score improvement notifications. Progress milestones.
MEDIUM Regulatory Changes
Risk: RBI regulation changes affecting data access or financial advice boundaries.
Mitigation: Conservative positioning of ARTH.OS as financial awareness, not advice. Legal counsel.
MEDIUM Category Awareness Gap
Risk: Users do not understand what a Financial Cognition System is, making acquisition difficult.
Mitigation: Lead with emotional outcomes ("Know your Survival Window"), not category labels.
LOW Large Platform Entry
Risk: Google, Paytm, or HDFC builds a similar product.
Mitigation: Proprietary dataset moat. BAS™ depth. Longitudinal relationships. Speed of category leadership.
LOW Monetisation Delay
Risk: Product proves conceptual value but struggles to convert engagement into revenue.
Mitigation: B2B2C model — selling BAS™ intelligence to lenders and insurers as revenue bridge.

I started SANKHYA because I was tired of watching people around me make the same financial mistakes,
over and over, not because they were unintelligent or irresponsible — but because no product in existence
gave them honest, precise, compassionate visibility into what was actually happening in their financial lives.

of financial outcomes. Behaviour and awareness were the real variables. And no one was measuring them.
ARTH.OS is my attempt to fix that. Not just for a niche. For everyone. For the person earning n12,000 a
